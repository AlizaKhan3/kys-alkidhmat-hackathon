So basically, I started my own small food business called Smash & Sauce Kitchen.

I’m basically a university student, and I’ve always had a huge passion for cooking. That’s actually the main reason why I decided to start this business.

I especially love making pasta. I’ve been making pasta for around four to five years now, and Alhamdulillah, I’ve received really good reviews from people who have tried it.

I also really enjoy making beef burgers, and it’s been almost one year since I started making them.

Of course, running a food business comes with its own challenges. It’s not always easy, and sometimes we do face losses, but that’s part of running a business. We try to manage everything as much as we can, and I also keep track of our expenses and losses in a diary.

I started this business with my aunt, and we are 50-50 partners. She mainly invests in the business, while I handle the actual work and operations, especially the cooking and orders.

When we first started, we used to get around two to three orders a week. Since it was a new business, we also tried getting some reviews from local people.

After that, we started getting a few more orders, and within our first one and a half months, we completed around 20 orders.

One thing I’ve realized recently is how different our purchasing process has become.

When we first started, we used to buy almost everything from supermarkets like Imtiaz, Naheed, and Chase.

But now that the business is becoming a little more organized, we buy our ingredients from specific brands and vendors.

For our burgers, we usually use Dawn and Bake Parlour products.

For pasta, we use Bake Parlour and Reggia.

For chicken and meat, we have our own vendor from whom we regularly purchase.

For fries, we initially used normal potatoes, but now we use 9mm potatoes.

For burger packaging, we use foil paper, and for pasta, we have 500 ml and 1000 ml boxes available.

Our beef burgers start from around Rs. 750, while our 500 ml pasta is Rs. 699.

We also offer homemade masala fries.

Another major challenge for me is managing the business alongside university.

I’m a university student, and usually my classes are from around 9 to 2 or sometimes 9 to 3.

So the biggest problem happens when we receive orders around lunchtime, because I’m usually not at home during that time.

If the order is pre-booked, then I usually prepare things the night before so that everything is ready.

But if we receive an order at that exact time, my sister or someone from my family receives the order through our WhatsApp or business number.

The problem is that I’m usually the one who actually prepares the food.

For example, if there’s a pasta order or we need to prepare our pasta sauces or even for burger we have to  prepare burger sauce , it can be difficult for my aunt to make everything exactly the way I do.

So most of the time, I have to call her and explain the recipe step by step.

But even then, sometimes there can be confusion, and in a food business, consistency is really important.

Even a small difference in the quantity of ingredients, sauce, or cooking time can change the taste.

And that’s honestly one of the biggest challenges I face while running a home-based food business as a university student.

But at the same time, I’m learning a lot through this experience — from costing and inventory to customer handling, consistency, and managing a business alongside my studies.

And honestly, I really enjoy it because this business started from something I genuinely love — cooking.













 Our First Investment

When we made our first investment, we actually did not face any loss.

Around one month and fifteen days later, we were able to recover around 40% of the amount that we had invested.

We also had a separate amount set aside for resale and other related expenses, so overall, our first investment worked out fine for us.

So, our first investment was actually a good learning experience, and we did not consider it a loss.

---

 Our Habitt Stall Experience

The major loss we experienced was during our Habitt stall.

For the stall, we had made a proper separate investment. We had planned everything separately, including the stock, preparation, and other expenses required for the stall.

But unfortunately, the sales we made were not enough to cover the overall investment and expenses.

Because we had prepared and purchased things specifically for the stall, a significant amount of our investment ended up becoming a loss.

There were also some issues related to the information and guidance we received from the organizers. The guidance and understanding we had regarding certain things were not accurate, and that contributed to the problems we faced during the stall.

So, overall, the Habitt stall was where we experienced a significant financial loss.

However, it was also my first experience of doing a proper food stall, so despite the loss, I learned a lot from it.

It taught me about event planning, estimating sales, managing stock, understanding the organizers' requirements, calculating investment beforehand, and most importantly, not over-preparing or over-investing without having a realistic estimate of the expected sales.

 Our Orders & Customers

Another thing I noticed when we first started was that most of our orders were coming from people we already knew — mainly our immediate family, friends, and people connected to them.

Getting orders from completely new or unknown customers was much less common in the beginning.

So one of the things we are trying to work on now is reaching more people outside our immediate circle and building a customer base that comes to us because they genuinely like the food and want to order from us again.

That is also one of the reasons why customer reviews and feedback are important for us.

Because for a small home-based food business, building trust with people who don't personally know you takes time.

And that's something we're slowly working on with Smash & Sauce Kitchen.

 Our Beef Patty

Now coming to our actual food preparation, especially our beef burger.

For our patty, we use our own meat mixture and prepare it according to our required quantity. We usually make enough mixture so that our overall stock can be properly managed.

For the patty mixture, we start with butter. Then we add garlic and ginger paste, along with our other basic seasonings.

We try to keep the base of our patty relatively simple because we want to maintain the original beef flavour.

But because some customers prefer a slightly spicy patty, we add a small amount of red chilli according to the quantity of meat. For example, when we're preparing one kilogram of meat, we add a controlled amount of red chilli.

We also add butter to the mixture in the required quantity.

I’ll be explaining the exact recipe and measurements for one kilogram of meat separately, because that will make it easier to understand exactly how much of each ingredient we use.

 Our Burger Sauce

For our burger sauce, we use mayonnaise, dippit bbq sauce, mustard sauce ,Worcestershire sauce, and tomato ketchup.

The brands we use can also make a big difference. If you use low-quality brands, the taste can change significantly.

So eventually, we started using different sauces and ingredients to create the flavour we actually wanted.

Apart from barbecue sauce, we also use mustard sauce, rosemary sauce, chilli sauce, paprika powder, rosemary, and black pepper.

These ingredients together make up our burger sauce.

 Our Pasta

For pasta, we usually boil the pasta for around 8–10 minutes, depending on the type and brand we are using.

As I mentioned earlier, we use Bake Parlour and Reggia pasta.

For our pasta sauce, we use cheddar cheese, specifically Adam's cheese, along with cream, milk, and butter.

We also use all-purpose milk for our pasta sauce.

The quality of the ingredients is really important here because changing the brand or using a lower-quality product can affect the overall taste and consistency of the pasta.

---

 A Problem We Faced With Our Patty

Sometimes our patty breaks while we're frying it.

One reason this can happen is when we accidentally add too much butter to the patty mixture.

Another situation is when we need to prepare the meat quickly. Sometimes we use a small amount of raw papaya paste to help tenderize the meat.

But if too much raw papaya paste is added, the mixture can release water. Because of that excess moisture, the patty can become weak and start breaking while frying.

We actually experienced this exact problem at our Habitt stall.

 Cheese We Use

For our burgers, we use Adam's burger cheese.

I personally prefer Adam's because I find its quality and melting consistency good.

For our pasta, we also use Adam's cheddar cheese.

 Our Chicken Preparation

For the chicken, we usually marinate it properly before preparing it.

For the marinade, we use Shan Chicken Tikka Powder, along with chicken powder, chilli sauce, soy sauce, black pepper, a little salt, chilli flakes, and oregano.

Depending on what we're preparing, we also use different seasoning mixes. Sometimes we use Rosemary products or Falak products.

The exact ingredients and quantities can vary depending on the recipe we're making.

---

 Our Homemade Pickles

For our burgers, we also make our own homemade pickles.

We take cucumber and slice it properly.

Then, in around one cup of hot water, we add approximately 3–4 tablespoons of vinegar, one small lemon, 2 tablespoons of sugar, and half a teaspoon of salt.

We put the sliced cucumbers into this mixture and leave them for around 30 minutes.

After that, we let the pickles cool down in the same liquid.

Once they have cooled, we store them in the refrigerator.

This is how we prepare our homemade pickles, and keeping them refrigerated helps us preserve them properly.
